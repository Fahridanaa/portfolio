### The problem

The Department of Information Technology needed one place to see which rooms were free and to handle booking requests from students and lecturers. The final project for our Object-Oriented Programming course was to build it.

### What I built

Orang Dalam Ruangan is a PHP application with its own small MVC core: a router, a controller base, PDO models, and session auth with a role check. There is no PHP framework in the stack.

A user signs in as a student or a lecturer and sees a floor plan. Each room shows its state from the class schedule and the confirmed bookings: free, waiting for approval, or in use. The booking form runs in five steps: choose an event or a class, fill in the details, pick a room on the plan, confirm, and finish. An event request carries an ID card, and the admin can require a letter before approving; an urgent request needs a separate proof document. There is also a class-move flow: a student or lecturer asks to move a scheduled session, and the other side accepts or rejects it.

The admin side is the other half: a queue of requests with the uploaded documents, approve or reject with a note, a history of past bookings, and an inbox of notifications. Every decision writes a notification for the student or lecturer who asked.

I wrote 61 of the repository's 90 non-merge commits. That covers the router, the auth and role middleware, the five-step form, the room detail and floor-plan views, and a large part of the models and admin views.

### The result

The result is a complete flow, not a CRUD demo: a user can find a free room, file a request with the documents, and track the decision; an admin can approve or reject it with the evidence in view. It was the final project for the course, and the repository is public.
